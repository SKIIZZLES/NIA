NIA

Community guidelines

Android app app.nia.mobile — last updated: 30 September 2026

In short: NIA is for sharing cultures and talent, with respect for everyone. You must be at least 13. No nudity and no sexual content are allowed, anywhere. Any content that sexualises a child is removed, the account deleted and the facts reported to the authorities.

Contact and reports from outside the app: niaapp@outlook.com

These guidelines explain in plain words what the Terms of Service provide. They apply to everything posted on NIA: videos, photos, captions, hashtags, comments, usernames, profile pictures, bios, sounds, series, events and lives.

1. Who can use NIA

  • You must be at least 13, or the legal minimum age in your country if it is higher.
  • If we learn that an account belongs to a child under 13, we delete it.
  • An account deleted for breaking these rules must not be recreated to get around the decision.

2. Banned content

These are the categories you will find when reporting content. They are listed from most to least serious.

2.1 Child sexual abuse and endangering minors — zero tolerance

Any image, video, text or link that shows, describes, sexualises or exploits a minor; soliciting a minor (grooming), sextortion, asking for intimate images, sharing such content, even "to denounce it". See our child safety standards.

2.2 Threats and immediate danger

Threats of death or violence, calls to harm someone, praise of or incitement to terrorism, promotion of suicide, self-harm or eating disorders, dangerous challenges.

2.3 Anti-Black racism (négrophobie)

Any statement, image, "joke" or caricature that demeans, dehumanises or excludes Black people, including slurs, animal comparisons and colourist stereotypes.

2.4 Racism and hate

Hate, discrimination or dehumanisation targeting a person or group because of their origin, ethnicity, nationality, religion, sex, disability or illness; denial or glorification of crimes against humanity.

2.5 Homophobic and transphobic speech

Slurs, mockery, calls for violence or exclusion targeting a person because of their sexual orientation or gender identity.

2.6 Inhumane acts

Torture, cruelty to people or animals, gratuitous violence, images of bodies or injuries shown to shock or glorify, glorification of crimes.

2.7 Nudity and sexual content — banned everywhere

No nudity and no sexual content are allowed on NIA, whatever the age of the person posting or watching, and even with the 18+ label described below. This includes pornography, real or simulated sexual acts, visible intimate parts, sexually suggestive content, offering or selling sexual services, and intimate images shared without consent.

2.8 Insults and harassment

Insults, humiliation, intimidation, pile-ons, publishing other people's private information (address, phone, documents), impersonation to cause harm.

2.9 Spam and manipulation

Repetitive or unsolicited promotional messages, scams, phishing, fake giveaways, buying or artificially trading likes, followers or views.

2.10 Other illegal or unfair content

Copyright infringement, sale of illegal or regulated goods (drugs, weapons), false information presented as verified in order to cause harm, and any other illegal content.

3. Lawful mature content and the 18+ label

Some lawful themes can be hard for a young audience: rites and scarification, circumcision, ritual slaughter, war violence or documentary footage, crude language. The 18+ label lets you restrict such content to adults: when posting a video or starting a live, an adult can tick “18+ content”. The content is then only shown to adults who chose to show 18+ content (Profile → Age and 18+ content, off by default); it is never shown to minors, and it cannot be reposted. Moderation can also apply this label.

This label is only for those lawful mature themes. It never allows nudity or sexual content, and does not make content banned under section 2 acceptable. Even when labelled 18+, post these themes with context (explanation, cultural or documentary framing) and without trying to shock.

4. How to report

Reports are anonymous to the person reported: they are not told who reported them.

WhatHow
A video or photoTap ⋯ on the post, then Report, and pick the reason.
A commentTap the flag next to the comment, then pick the reason.
A profileOpen the profile, tap the flag at the top right, then pick the reason.
Without an account, or any other issueWrite to niaapp@outlook.com with the username involved, a description of the content and, if possible, its date. Anyone can report, even without a NIA account.

You can also block an account from its profile: its videos disappear from your feed.

Child sexual abuse material: do not download it, screenshot it or share it, even to denounce it. Report it in the app and to the authorities — in France, on PHAROS (internet-signalement.gouv.fr), the official platform for reporting illegal online content.

Child in danger (France): call 119 (free, 24/7). Immediate danger: call 17 or 112. Cyberbullying: 3018 (free, anonymous). Elsewhere, contact the police or child protection services in your country.

5. What we do with a report

  • Every report is reviewed by a person, not only by a machine. Reports involving a minor or a serious threat get top priority.
  • Depending on severity, we may remove the content, restrict or suspend the account, or delete it.
  • Any child sexual abuse material is removed, the account deleted and the facts reported to the competent authorities, including PHAROS.
  • A threat to someone's life or safety may be passed on to the authorities.

6. Appealing a decision

If you think content was removed or an account sanctioned by mistake, write to niaapp@outlook.com with your username and the content involved. We review the case and reply.

7. Changes to these guidelines

These guidelines may change as the app evolves. Any change is published on this page with a new update date; major changes are announced in the app.
